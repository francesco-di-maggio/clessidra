Design

How the firmware works. Every value is the firmware's.

Signal Path

  1. 01 InputLeft and right summed to mono, DC removed
  2. 02 FillBlends the input into the buffer
  3. 03 Buffer8 s, mono, 48 kHz
  4. 04 Grains32 voices, up to 131 grains per second
  5. 05 NormaliserBack to the input's level
  6. 06 ReverbFollows Pour
  7. 07 LimiterLook-ahead 5 ms, ceiling −0.5 dBFS
  8. 08 OutputStereo

The Buffer

One buffer holds the sound: 8 s, mono, in the Daisy's SDRAM. Fill writes into it, the grains read from it. They read in two places: the selection, which the playhead moves through, and the input window, just behind the write point. Position (S30) sets how far back the selection lies from where the held sound ends, Length (S35) how long it is.

Fill

Each sample written is Fill × input + (1 − Fill) × what was there. The write point moves only while Fill is up. Each grain reads the held sound or follows the input, and Fill sets the odds.

FillGrains following the inputWritten
0 %NoneNothing, the write point stops. The slices are found.
10 %1 in 1010 % new, 90 % old
50 %1 in 2Half new, half old
100 %AllAll new. The held sound ends at the write point.

The last 2 % at each end are exact: sealed, or only the input. Each time the write point passes, what was there keeps (1 − Fill) of its level. Coming down from 100 %, the held sound ends at that moment and plays from the start of the selection.

Pour

One fader opens the glass, from single drops to a cloud. Density, grain size, window and reverb all follow it. Size (S31) moves the grain size 2 octaves either way, and a firm press on a slice opens Pour further.

Grains per second rise exponentially. Reverb is its decay time, and its share of the sound falls as Pour opens. From 60 % the grains start to differ by themselves, up to Scatter at 0.3 at the top.

A Grain

PositionThe playhead's point, plus Scatter's reach: up to ±2 s, cubic. Stays inside what is playing, mirrored at its ends
PitchS33. Above Scatter's centre, random pitch: most grains near the pitch, a few up to ±24 semitones
SizePour and S31, 10 ms to half the sound, at most 4 s. With Scatter, each from half to twice
WindowFrom Pour: percussive to Hann
PanEqual power. The width grows with the grains sounding and with Scatter
DirectionThe playhead's
LengthThe same at any pitch

Starts and Voices

StartsGrains per second, whatever their size. Scatter at 0: periodic. Up: random intervals, without the closest starts and the longest gaps, at the same mean
Voices32. Over 24 sounding, the one furthest along fades out in 5 ms, so a new grain always starts
OverlapAt high density the grains shorten, so 24 voices hold whole windows: 183 ms at the top of Pour

Slices

When Fill seals the sound, the firmware cuts it into 7 slices at its onsets: a rise of 6 dB over the 30 ms before, in 5 ms frames, above −50 dBFS, at least 50 ms apart. The strongest onsets are kept, and each slice starts 5 ms before its onset. With too few onsets, the longest slices are halved. While the input comes in, the slices are equal parts of the input window.

P03–P09 play the slices, first to last: left to right on the panel, top to bottom within a column. A touch plays one from its start. A firm press opens Pour towards the top. Released, the whole selection plays again, once a one-shot has finished its slice.

Tilt and End

TiltCentre stops. Up forward, down backward. Speed rises with the square of the distance: 1 halfway, with a detent, 4 at the end. Crossing the centre flips
One-shotS07 up. Stops at the end of the selection or slice, until a slice or a flip restarts it
TurnS07 centre. Turns at each end
LoopS07 down. Wraps round. Grains following the input always do

Level

NormaliserCopies of one sound add up in amplitude, independent grains in power. Both are measured, smoothed over 5 ms, and the sum is brought back to the input's level with 0.9 headroom. Grains that cancel are not boosted
Limiter+6 dB in. Look-ahead 5 ms, ceiling −0.5 dBFS
LEDGlows with the grains. A 300 ms pulse when a control reaches its centre

Runtime

BoardDaisy Seed. 48 kHz, 48-sample blocks (1 ms). Pads read every 3 ms
